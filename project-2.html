Assistive Autonomous Robot

The objective was to design and construct an autonomous robot that replicates a real-world application: an assistive robot in Germany that enters a kitchen, turns off a stove, finds a plate, and returns it to an elderly person. For this project, the stove was represented by a 1 kHz red beacon, and the plate by a 10 kHz green beacon.

Project Overview

The primary objective of the Robot Project was to design, build, and program an autonomous robot capable of replicating tasks inspired by a real-world German autonomous robot assisting the elderly.The robot had to perform these tasks within a bordered arena without team assistance, while adhering to constraints such as a weight limit (under 2 kg for extra points) and completing the tasks as quickly as possible.

My primary role was soldering the Infrared Receiver Board (IRB), the robot’s sensory “eyes,” as well as building a mechanically robust design through the Vex squarebot materials. My soldering work on the IRB was intricate and pivotal, requiring me to assemble components like resistors, LEDs, and sensors under tight time constraints with limited availability on tools. I navigated challenges such as inconsistent tools—a flat chisel tip that spread heat unevenly and a needle-nose tip that concentrated it too much—adapting to what was available as other students competed for resources. A significant hurdle arose when the LED indicators, meant to signal code execution, failed to stay powered, indicating a soldering flaw. After unsuccessful attempts to replace a potentially faulty component, I identified the issue and secured a new, fully functional IRB, meticulously soldering it to ensure reliable beacon detection.

Our efforts culminated in a robot that excelled, completing all tasks in a swift 19.1 seconds and weighing just 1.98 kg, earning bonus points for both speed and weight. The soldering precision enabled flawless beacon detection, while the mechanical build’s lightweight yet sturdy design—refined through countless tweaks like lengthening the arm and adjusting the beacon catcher—ensured smooth operation. This project deepened my technical skills in soldering and mechanical construction, teaching me the importance of adaptability, teamwork, and iterative refinement. Though we noted potential enhancements, such as stabilizing the arm further or perfecting dual ultrasonic sensors, our success exceeded expectations
